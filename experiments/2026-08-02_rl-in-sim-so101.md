# RL in simulation for SO-101 — grasp and lift

**Status**: 📋 **Planned — not started.** No code, no runs. · **Owner**: unassigned · **Created**: 2026-08-02

> Queued deliberately behind [ACT wrist-only](2026-07-30_act-wrist-only-so101.md). Imitation on the real arm is the club's first baseline; this is the second track, and it should not compete for the arm.

## The question

**Can we train a grasp-and-lift policy for the SO-101 in simulation and have it work on our actual arm?**

Sub-question that matters more for the club: **is simulation worth the setup cost at all**, given we have one arm and four founders?

## Why do this

Ranked by honest value to us, which is *not* the order you'd guess:

1. **Parallelism.** One arm, four people. Sim lets three of us work while one has the hardware. This is the real reason.
2. **Safety and practice.** A member can drive the arm into a singularity, crash the gripper, and learn from it at zero cost.
3. **Eval scenarios we can't safely run on hardware** — hundreds of randomized object positions, including ones that would collide. Directly feeds the evaluation problem in [training/README](../docs/training/README.md).
4. **A better policy than ACT.** Listed last on purpose. **We should not expect this**, and the experiment is worth running even if RL never beats our imitation baseline.

## Prerequisites

- [ ] MJCF model pinned (see the calibration trap below)
- [ ] A CUDA box. **Not the Mac cockpit** — the [ACT throughput benchmark](2026-07-30_act-wrist-only-so101.md) already showed the M4 Air is compute-bound for training. Explorer (H200/A100), env built on a compute node into `/scratch`.
- [ ] Working real-arm teleop + a recorded dataset, so there is something to compare against

## Three routes, and which one we take

| Route | Stack | Evidence it works | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. MuJoCo + SB3** | Menagerie MJCF + Stable-Baselines3 SAC, hand-written env and reward | [ggando's post](https://ggando.com/blog/so101-rl-lift/): **100% success in sim**, ~4 h on one AMD GPU. **No sim-to-real result** — author says *"I don't know yet"*. No domain randomization mentioned. | **Read it, don't rebuild it.** Best teaching artifact available; weakest path to a working policy. |
| **B. ManiSkill3 + Squint** | GPU-parallel sim, visual SAC | [Squint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21203) (Almuzairee & Christensen, Feb 2026): **8-task SO-101 suite, heavy domain randomization, sim-to-real transfer to a real SO-101 demonstrated, 15 min on one RTX 3090** (most tasks <6 min). | ✅ **Start here.** Dominates A on every axis: more tasks, actual transfer, ~16× faster. |
| **C. Real-robot RL, no sim** | HIL-SERL — **already installed** in our LeRobot 0.6.0 | [ggando's Feb post](https://ggando.com/blog/so101-hil-serl/) does real-world grasping from scratch on an SO-101. | **The finishing step**, not the start. No sim-to-real gap because there's no sim; cost is wall-clock and hardware wear. |

### What we already have (verified in the `phi` env, 2026-08-02)
LeRobot 0.6.0 ships a complete **real-robot** RL stack, so Route C needs no new dependencies:
- `lerobot/rl/algorithms/sac/` — **SAC is the only algorithm shipped**
- `lerobot/rl/gym_manipulator.py` — wraps the real arm as a Gym env
- `lerobot/rl/{actor,learner,learner_service,buffer,queue,trainer}.py` — distributed actor/learner
- `lerobot/rewards/classifier/` — a **learned reward model**, so you train a success/failure image classifier instead of hand-writing nine reward terms
- `lerobot/processor/hil_processor.py` — human-in-the-loop
- `lerobot/policies/tdmpc` — model-based RL, for the world-model track

⚠️ **LeRobot ships no SO-101 simulator.** `lerobot/envs/` has `libero`, `metaworld`, `robocasa`, `robomme`, `robotwin`, `vlabench` — benchmark suites only, no MuJoCo SO-101 and no ManiSkill. Simulation must come from outside LeRobot.

## 🚨 Traps to settle before writing any code

_Rechecked against upstream on 2026-08-05. Two of the original entries were wrong; one new one found._

**1. The MJCF calibration variant.** ✅ Real, but **not where this doc said it was.**

[MuJoCo Menagerie](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie) `trs_so_arm100/` contains
**only** `so_arm100.xml` + `scene.xml` — the 5-DoF SO-**100**. No SO-101, no calibration variants.
The two variants live in **[TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100 `Simulation/SO101/`](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/tree/main/Simulation/SO101)**:
- `so101_new_calib.xml` (default) — each joint's virtual zero is the **middle of its range**
- `so101_old_calib.xml` — zero is the **fully-extended horizontal** pose

Switch by editing the included robot file in `scene.xml`.

This is the sim-to-real form of the bug class in [01-hardware](../docs/robots/so-arm101/01-hardware.md): LeRobot normalizes each joint against *that physical arm's* measured range, while the MJCF uses one of two fixed conventions. **Mismatch them and identical action values mean different physical angles in sim and on hardware. Nothing errors.** Pin the variant, record it here, and check it against our actual calibration before trusting any transferred policy.

**2. The gripper is a linear joint in LeRobot and is not modelled that way in the MJCF.** 🆕

Straight from the upstream README: in LeRobot the gripper is a **linear joint** where `0` = fully
closed and `100` = fully open, and this mapping *"is not yet reflected in the current URDF and
MuJoCo files."* Our action vector is 6-dim **including** the gripper, so this is not a corner case —
it is dimension 6 of every action we send. Anything hand-built on these MJCFs must convert the
gripper channel explicitly.

**3. ~~Cartesian vs joint action space~~ — resolved; the concern does not apply to Route B.**

The original worry came from ggando's MuJoCo policy, which outputs Cartesian XYZ through IK. But
**Squint is joint-space.** Its SO-101 agent (`envs/robot/so101.py`) registers only joint controllers
— `pd_joint_delta_pos` (default), `pd_joint_pos`, `pd_joint_target_delta_pos`, `pd_joint_vel` — over
6 active joints, delta limits ±0.1 rad per arm joint and ±0.2 for the gripper. Its own comment notes
`pd_joint_vel` is *"Not supported on real SO101."*

So a Squint policy **is** comparable to our joint-space ACT baseline, modulo delta-vs-absolute
targets. Choosing Route B makes the action-space decision for us.

## Verified 2026-08-05: Squint deploys *through* LeRobot

The finding that most changes the cost estimate. `deploy_utils/robot_config.py` builds the real robot
with **our own stack**:

```python
from lerobot.robots.so_follower.config_so_follower import SO101FollowerConfig
from lerobot.cameras.opencv.configuration_opencv import OpenCVCameraConfig
from lerobot.robots.utils import make_robot_from_config
```

and reads a `calibration_dir` — i.e. **the SO-101 calibration file we already have.** Its
`LeRobotRealAgent` is adapted from ManiSkill's own `agents/robots/lerobot/manipulator.py`, wrapping a
LeRobot `Robot` behind ManiSkill's `BaseRealAgent` interface.

**Every one of those imports resolves in our installed lerobot 0.6.0** (checked in the `phi` env,
2026-08-05). There is no adapter to write. Only the sim side (`mani_skill`, `sapien`) is missing —
nothing sim-related is installed today.

## Verified 2026-08-05: Explorer can probably render; the login node cannot

ManiSkill renders through SAPIEN, which needs a **Vulkan** driver, and every Vulkan install command in
its docs needs `sudo`, which we do not have. So this was the real gate, and it is *mostly* clear:

| | Login node (`explorer-01`) | GPU node (`d1002`) |
|---|---|---|
| `/usr/share/vulkan/icd.d/nvidia_icd*.json` | ❌ absent (Intel/AMD/lavapipe only) | ✅ **present** |
| `/usr/share/glvnd/egl_vendor.d/10_nvidia.json` | ❌ absent (Mesa only) | ✅ **present** |
| `libGLX_nvidia` in `ldconfig` | ❌ | ✅ |
| `nvidia_layers.json` implicit layer | ❌ | ❌ **absent** (only Mesa device-select) |

Driver 545.23.08, consistent with [hpc/explorer](../docs/hpc/explorer.md).

**Necessary but not sufficient.** The ICD and EGL vendor files exist on compute nodes, so the usual
`enumeratePhysicalDevices: ErrorInitializationFailed` cause is absent. But `nvidia_layers.json` —
which ManiSkill's docs call out as needed *"for some GPUs like A100"*, and we have A100s — is missing
and we cannot install it. **Files present ≠ rendering works.** The only proof is running
`python -m mani_skill.examples.demo_random_action` inside a GPU job, and that is step 0 of this
experiment. If it fails on A100 and works on V100, pin the node type.

Fallback if SAPIEN cannot render on Explorer at all: **MuJoCo MJX**, which does not need Vulkan. That
costs us Squint and the 8-task suite and puts us back to hand-writing an env — Route A with extra
steps.

## Verified 2026-08-05: what Squint actually trains on

Read from `train_squint.py` defaults, because the numbers are the surprising part:

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Parallel envs | **1024** |
| Total timesteps | **1,500,000** |
| Render resolution | 128 × 128 |
| **Policy input resolution** | **16 × 16** |
| Obs mode | `rgb+segmentation` |
| Batch size | 512 |
| GPU | RTX 3080 or better, ≥10 GB |

**The name is literal: "resolution squinting" means the policy sees 16×16 images.** Renders come out
at 128×128 and are area-downsampled to 16×16 before the encoder; the repo carries separate CNN
encoders for 64/32/16 and errors on anything else. Compare our ACT, which consumes 640×480 and
spends 300 tokens per camera on it.

The randomization is per-episode and per-step, not a fixed texture shuffle: gripper stiffness and
damping, ambient lighting, robot colour, camera position/rotation/FOV noise (±1° rot, ±5° FOV
third-person; ±1° and ±0.002 m wrist), and a background overlay composited behind the scene —
default `black_overlay.png`, i.e. the greenscreen trick with a black plate.

**Contrast with [lerobot-sim2real](https://github.com/StoneT2000/lerobot-sim2real)** (PPO, the other
credible route): 1024 envs but **100,000,000** timesteps, ~1 h on an RTX 4090, with the tutorial
saying 25-40M is the minimum that deploys. Squint reaches its tasks in **1.5M** — roughly 60× fewer
environment steps, because SAC is off-policy and reuses every transition while PPO throws its batch
away. That ratio is the single strongest argument for Route B over the PPO tutorial, and it is also a
warning: 1024 envs × cheap steps means the bottleneck is **rendering throughput**, not the network.

## Plan

_Revised 2026-08-05 after reading the upstream code. Step 0 is new and gates everything._

0. **Prove SAPIEN renders on Explorer.** One GPU job: install `mani_skill` into a `/scratch` env on a
   compute node, run `python -m mani_skill.examples.demo_random_action`. Try both a V100 and an A100
   node, because `nvidia_layers.json` is missing. **If this fails, the whole ManiSkill route is
   dead** and we fall back to MJX. Cheapest possible go/no-go — do it before anything else.
1. **Reproduce `SO101LiftCube-v1`** with `python train_squint.py --env_id=SO101LiftCube-v1`. Claimed
   ~3 min on a 3090; on an H200 it should not be worse. Success = their curve on our cluster.
2. ~~Decide the action space~~ — decided: joint-space `pd_joint_delta_pos`, inherited from Squint.
3. **Match the real camera to the sim camera** with `deploy_utils/tune_camera.py`. This is the step
   both upstream projects call the hardest part; lerobot-sim2real says outright *"this is the hardest
   part of the tutorial."* Our [`camera_realign`](../src/phi/utils/camera_realign.py) blend view is
   the same idea and the same discipline, so we are not starting cold.
4. **Point `robot_config.py` at our arm and our existing calibration dir.** Note it defaults to a
   single `base_camera`; our rig has three, and the swapped-key problem does **not** exist in sim, so
   keep sim naming physical and convert only at the LeRobot boundary.
5. **Attempt transfer.** `python deploy.py --checkpoint=... --env_id=SO101LiftCube-v1`. Report the
   real-arm success rate honestly, including zero.
6. **Only then** consider HIL-SERL to close the remainder.

**Read ggando's four posts alongside this, not before it.** They are the best teaching artifact on our
arm, but the code to actually run is Squint's, and one of the traps in this doc came from taking his
Cartesian choice as general.

## What counts as a result

Any of these is a publishable club result, including the negative ones:
- A transferred sim policy with a **measured real-arm success rate** — even 0%, if we can say why
- A **like-for-like comparison** against the ACT baseline on the same task, same bins
- A **usable sim environment** other members can run without the arm — this alone justifies the work

⚠️ **A sim-only success rate is not a result.** ggando's 100% in MuJoCo says nothing about hardware. Report real-arm numbers or report that we didn't get there.
